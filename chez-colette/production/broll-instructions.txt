Chez Colette. French decorating for ordinary homes, by a Lyon decorator who spent fourteen years in small, rented, badly lit flats. Present day, Europe.

THE SCRIPT CHOOSES THE ROOM — sitting room, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, hallway, storage, studio, whole flat. Follow it. These notes set style only, never subject.

SHOW: real lived-in European flats. Tall windows, shutters, parquet, plaster walls, radiators under the sill, picture rails. Curtains — poles, headings, drops to the floor. Table and floor lamps lit warm at night. Linen, wool, worn wood, unglazed ceramic, stone, a plant. Small fitted kitchens, worktops and wall cupboards. Small tiled bathrooms, a basin and a mirror. Narrow hallways with hooks. Beds against a wall, bedside lamps. Wardrobes, a clothes rail in a curtained alcove, shelves. Colour cards, a half-tested wall. Hands measuring, hanging, moving furniture off a wall. Rooms mid-correction, not finished. Lyon and the Croix-Rousse.

KEEP OUT: châteaux, mansions, luxury villas, show homes. Large American suburban homes — drywall, ceiling fans, open-plan island kitchens, walk-in dressing rooms. Showrooms and magazine perfection. Trend décor: arched mirrors, travertine tables, potted olive trees, bouclé chairs — the scripts argue against them. Flat overhead light, bright white midday rooms. Stock families. Any text, captions, numbers or graphics.

TONE: quiet, warm, slightly worn. Evening and low side light more than noon. Flats a normal person could afford.
