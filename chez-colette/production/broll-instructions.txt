Chez Colette. French interior decorating for ordinary homes, presented by a Lyon decorator who spent fourteen years fixing small, rented and badly lit flats. Present day, France and Europe.

SHOW: real lived-in European apartments — tall windows, shutters, parquet, plaster walls, radiators under the sill, a picture rail. Curtains: poles, headings, drops, folds, fabric hanging to the floor. Table lamps and floor lamps lit warm after dark, shadows across a room. Linen, wool, worn wood grain, unglazed ceramic, stone, a plant. Paint brushes, colour cards, a wall half tested. Hands measuring, hanging, adjusting, pulling furniture out from a wall. Rooms mid-correction rather than finished. Lyon, the Croix-Rousse, old silk-weavers' buildings with very tall windows.

KEEP OUT: châteaux, mansions, luxury villas, designer show homes. American suburban interiors, drywall, ceiling fans, kitchen islands, walk-in closets. Furniture showrooms and staged magazine perfection. Trend décor — arched mirrors, travertine tables, potted olive trees, bouclé chairs — the script argues against exactly these. Flat overhead lighting and bright white midday rooms. Smiling stock-photo families. Any text, captions, numbers, arrows or graphics on screen.

TONE: quiet, warm, slightly worn. Evening and low side light far more often than noon. Ordinary flats a normal person could actually afford to live in.
