Chez Colette. French decorating for ordinary homes, by a Lyon decorator who spent fourteen years fixing small, rented, badly lit flats. Present day, France and Europe.

THE SCRIPT CHOOSES THE ROOM — sitting room, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, hallway, studio, whole flat. Follow it. These notes set the style only, never the subject.

SHOW: real lived-in European flats. Tall windows, shutters, parquet, plaster walls, radiators under the sill, picture rails. Curtains — poles, headings, drops, folds to the floor. Table and floor lamps lit warm at night. Linen, wool, worn wood, unglazed ceramic, stone, a plant. Small fitted kitchens with worktops and wall cupboards. Small tiled bathrooms, a basin and a mirror. Narrow hallways with hooks. Beds against a wall, bedside lamps. Brushes, colour cards, a half-tested wall. Hands measuring, hanging, pulling furniture off a wall. Rooms mid-correction, not finished. Lyon and the Croix-Rousse.

KEEP OUT: châteaux, mansions, luxury villas, show homes. Large American suburban interiors — drywall, ceiling fans, open-plan island kitchens, walk-in closets. Showrooms and staged magazine perfection. Trend décor: arched mirrors, travertine tables, potted olive trees, bouclé chairs — the scripts argue against these. Flat overhead lighting, bright white midday rooms. Stock-photo families. Any text, captions, numbers or graphics on screen.

TONE: quiet, warm, slightly worn. Evening and low side light far more than noon. Flats a normal person could afford.
