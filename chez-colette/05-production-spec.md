# Production Spec — Chez Colette

## 1. Editing style

Measured by watching Bertha's shower video and Elias's mosquito video frame by frame. The two are
near-identical, which is more evidence of one shared production template. Copy it.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Avatar on screen | **15–30% of runtime** |
| Full-screen b-roll | **60–75%** |
| Split screen (avatar + b-roll) | **10–13%** |
| On-screen text, subtitles, arrows, lower thirds, chapter cards | **none. zero.** |
| B-roll clip length | **2–4 seconds** |
| Transitions | **100% hard cuts.** No crossfades, ever. |
| Cut timing | on sentence starts and subject changes, not to a music grid |
| Music | one low, somber, acoustic bed — guitar, piano, light strings. Never changes, never swells. |
| Sound effects | none |
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 |
| Colour grade | naturalistic, slightly desaturated. Warm for interiors, cooler for the "problem" shots. |

**The most important number on this page is the first one.** Your avatar is a still image with AI
lip-sync. Keeping her on screen under 30% of the time is not a stylistic choice, it is what makes
the channel watchable. B-roll is camouflage. Rapid 2–4 second cuts keep the eye moving so it never
settles long enough to notice the frozen hair, the static clothing, or the lip-sync.

Two avatar treatments both work: Bertha's is a completely fixed frame; Elias's has a continuous,
extremely slow digital push-in for the whole video. **Use the slow push-in.** It does more to make a
still image feel alive, and it costs nothing.

**The no-subtitles rule is counter to nearly all YouTube advice, and every one of these channels
follows it.** Burned-in captions and text overlays read as "content." Their absence reads as
documentary. Do not add them.

## 2. The avatar set

Your generated image is already better positioned than Bertha's, because every object in frame is a
b-roll prompt: fabric swatches, the lamp, the design sketches, wood samples, the ceramic mug, the
marble fireplace, the books, the plants.

- **Keep the set identical across every video.** Same room, same blazer, same table. Consistency is
  what makes a synthetic presenter read as a person with a home rather than a rendering.
- **Framing:** chest-up to waist-up, centred or slightly off-centre. Eyes and mouth clearly visible.
- **Generate 4–6 variant stills** of the same setup — holding a swatch, hand resting on the table,
  looking slightly down at a sketch, mid-gesture. Rotate between them at section breaks. This buys
  you a huge amount of perceived motion for almost no work and breaks the "one frozen photo"
  impression.
- **Split-screen layout:** avatar left, b-roll right (Elias's arrangement). Pick one side and never
  swap it.

## 3. B-roll

Your niche is far better served here than the cleaning channels. Interiors b-roll is abundant,
beautiful, and cheap.

**Sources:** licensed stock (Pexels, Envato, Artgrid) for real rooms, windows, fabric, light,
hands; AI-generated video for the specific "before" states you cannot find in stock — the badly-lit
room, the curtain stopping four inches short, the 5000K bulb; AI stills with a slow pan for
historical material (Haussmann interiors, Versailles parquet, toile de Jouy prints, a Croix-Rousse
loom atelier).

**Per video you need roughly 120–200 clips** at 2–4 seconds each for a 14-minute script. Build a
reusable library; most clips work across many videos.

**The shot list that matters most:** light falling across a wall · a hand touching fabric · a
curtain hem at the floor · a bulb being changed · a ceiling corner and its mouldings · parquet in
raking light · a window from inside, backlit · a lamp switching on in a dim room · paint being
brushed out · a tape measure against a wall · someone stepping back to look at a room.

**Always cut to the thing being named.** Colette says "the curtain stops four inches above the
floor" and the cut is a curtain hem four inches above a floor. This is what makes the format feel
like documentary rather than narration over wallpaper.

## 4. Thumbnails

**Read this honestly first:** I could not open the reference thumbnails. This sandbox's network
policy blocks YouTube's image CDN — `i.ytimg.com`, `img.youtube.com` and `i9.ytimg.com` all return
403 at the proxy. So I have not seen them with my own eyes and I am not going to describe them as
though I had.

What follows is split into what I actually verified and what is a genre-convention hypothesis. Spend
two minutes scrolling the five channels' video grids yourself and you will close this gap
completely — and you should, before committing to a template.

### Verified
- Every reference video is **over 10 minutes**; Bertha 9:48–17:10, Elias 14:15–35:03. Long-form
  signalling.
- Titles are **long** — frequently 60–95 characters, often with a parenthetical. This means the
  thumbnail is carrying an image, not a duplicate of the title, because the title is doing the
  verbal work on its own.
- Both channels' video interiors use a **naturalistic, slightly desaturated grade**, with the
  "problem" shots pushed grimmer and the "solution" shots warmer.
- The avatar is framed chest-up in a domestic setting in both.
- Bertha's channel sustains a single title bracket, `(The Mexican Way)`, across eleven-plus videos,
  which means the *series* is the recognisable unit.

### Hypothesis — treat as a starting point to test
A before/after or problem/solution split is the standard construction in this genre, with the
presenter's face on one side and the subject on the other, three to five words of large text
maximum, high contrast, and one saturated accent colour held consistent across the channel.

### What I would actually ship, and test
- **A consistent template, whatever it is.** The single biggest lever is that a returning viewer
  recognises a Chez Colette thumbnail in half a second. Pick a layout and hold it for 20 videos.
- **Colette's face in every thumbnail, same expression register**, mid-explanation, looking at
  camera. Faces outperform, and it compounds her into a recognisable person.
- **A split: the wrong room on one side, the corrected room on the other.** Your niche has the
  best visual before/after of any niche in this dataset — that is a real advantage over the
  cleaning channels, and you should press it hard.
- **Three to five words of text, maximum**, and never a duplicate of the title. The title says
  *"11 French Living Room Rules Americans Break"*; the thumbnail says *"WRONG"* / *"RIGHT"*, or
  *"4 INCHES"*, or nothing at all.
- **One accent colour for the whole channel.** Something French, warm and unusual against a
  YouTube feed of red and yellow: a deep ochre, or the Gris Trianon grey-green. It becomes your
  brand mark at thumbnail scale.
- **Test the arrow/circle annotation on the specific error.** Your content is diagnostic — "look at
  *this*" — and a single circle on a curtain hem or a light bulb is a genuinely informative
  thumbnail element rather than noise.

## 7. Cadence

Both reference channels batch-publish — many videos share a publish date. Bertha shipped **39 videos
in six months to 144K subs and 5.9M views.** That is roughly 6–7 per month in clusters.

**Build a backlog of 8–10 before you launch, then publish twice a week on a fixed schedule.** The
economics of this format depend on volume, because one 11x outlier pays for twenty average videos —
and you cannot know in advance which one it will be.

## 8. Voice model

She speaks English with a French accent. Whichever TTS you use:
- **Lock the voice and never change it.** A voice change mid-channel reads as a different person.
- Target **170–180 words per minute.** Bertha's measured average is 180; that is the right pace for
  this niche — unhurried but not slow.
- Test the French terms before committing. If the model mangles *badigeon à la chaux*, either
  respell it phonetically in the script or choose a different term. The project instructions already
  ask for pronunciation notes after each script for exactly this reason.
- Keep the music bed low enough that the accent stays fully intelligible. An accent plus a loud
  music bed costs you retention at exactly the moment you are trying to build trust.
